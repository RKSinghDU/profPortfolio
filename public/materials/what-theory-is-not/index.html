What Theory Is Not

A paper can have many references, strong data and a neat diagram, and still have no theory. Here is how to tell, and how to fix it.

References Data Lists of variables Diagrams Hypotheses

Based on Sutton, R. I., and Staw, B. M. (1995). What theory is not. Administrative Science Quarterly, 40(3), 371 to 384.
Explained for students by R K Singh, Senior Professor, Department of Commerce, Delhi School of Economics, University of Delhi.

The big idea

Theory is the answer to why

What you saw is one thing. Why it happens is another. Theory is the second.

What we observe: exits are higher among night shift agents at a call center in Gurugram.
Why?
Because night shifts cut agents off from friends, family and teammates, little ties them to the job, so a better offer meets almost no resistance.

What you saw is not theory

"Younger agents quit more" is something you observed. Theory explains why it happens.

A name is not an explanation

Writing "equity theory" is not enough. Say what the idea claims, in your own words, and how it applies to your case.

Because is the test

If your paragraph has no because and no so, it probably has no theory yet.

The call center example is the teacher's own, made up to show the difference. It is not from the article.

The five things

Five things that are not theory

Sutton and Staw say reviewers keep meeting these five in place of theory. Each one is useful. None of them is the why.

1 of 5

References are not theory

In the article: naming theories and listing citations is not the same as laying out the logic inside them. Adding more citations to answer "more theory, please" does not help.

Why it falls shortIt names ideas and sources but does not say how they lead to the result.
What to addThe borrowed logic in your own sentences, so a reader can follow without reading the source.
Not theory

"As equity theory and social exchange theory suggest, pay shapes attrition at Indian IT services firms."

Theory

"Because an engineer in Pune compares her pay with batchmates who joined elsewhere, a visible gap feels unfair, and leaving becomes a way to restore the balance."

2 of 5

Data are not theory

In the article: data show which patterns were seen. Theory explains why they occurred or why you expect them. A quote from an interview can inspire an idea, but the quote is not the theory.

Why it falls shortIt tells what happened, not why.
What to addThe reasons the pattern appeared, and the reasons you expect your own.
Not theory

"Younger employees at our Hyderabad unit left more often."

Theory

"Because younger employees carry fewer family obligations in the city, moving for a better offer costs them less, so exits rise as age falls."

3 of 5

Lists of variables are not theory

In the article: a list of variables, or a sorting of causes into groups, does not explain an outcome. A theory must say why variables matter and how they connect.

Why it falls shortIt lists what to measure, not how each item connects to the outcome.
What to addFor every variable, a reason it should matter, and how it relates to the others.
Not theory

"We include age, gender, tenure, pay, supervisor quality, commute time and city as determinants of retention."

Theory

"Because a supervisor controls the assignments that build an employee's reputation, supervisor quality matters more for retention than commute time, which only changes daily comfort."

4 of 5

Diagrams are not theory

In the article: boxes and arrows add order, but they rarely explain why the links will appear. Words are almost always needed. Good theory can be drawn, but it is rich enough to need sentences too.

Why it falls shortThe arrows show an order, not the reasons.
What to addOne or more sentences of reasoning behind every arrow.
Not theory

Figure 1: Shift timing leads to Stress, which leads to Quitting.

Theory

"Because night shifts cut agents off from the people who give support, stress builds with nobody to share it, and leaving becomes the way out."

The theory sentence in this box is the teacher's own.

5 of 5

Hypotheses are not theory

In the article: a hypothesis says what you expect, not why. It is a bridge between theory and data. Too many hypotheses, each with a thin reason, is a warning sign. Strong papers grow from one idea or a small set of ideas.

Why it falls shortIt states an expectation without a reason.
What to addA short paragraph of reasoning before each prediction.
Not theory

"H3: Founder involvement is negatively related to cross team cooperation in family firms."

Theory

"Because a founder personally decides pay, managers compete for the founder's attention instead of building ties with each other, so cooperation weakens as founder involvement grows."

Every Not theory and Theory sentence on this page is the teacher's own, written for practice. None is quoted from the article.

How to do it

Four steps to write the because

The authors say they offer no recipe for building theory. This routine is the teacher's own practical way to apply their advice. It is a help, not a rule from the article.

  1. Write what you expect, in one line.Night shift agents will quit more.
  2. Ask why, three times.Why? They feel cut off from people. Why does that matter? Fewer people would miss them or ask them to stay. Why does that matter? A better offer meets little resistance.
  3. Join the answers with because and so.Because night shifts cut agents off from the people around them, little ties them to the job, so a better offer meets almost no resistance.
  4. Say when it should hold more and when less.It should be weaker where a whole night batch are close friends.

What good work looks like

What rigorous work looks like

  • Every hypothesis has a short paragraph of reasons before it.
  • Each citation is explained in your own words: what it found, and why it matters for your argument.
  • Every arrow in a diagram has a sentence behind it.
  • You keep to one or two core ideas, not a long list of separate predictions.
  • You say when your idea should hold more and when less.
  • Your own new idea is called a proposal, not a finding, and you never claim something about earlier studies that you have not checked.

The first five points follow the article. The last point is the teacher's addition.

Practice

Write the because

Write your own reason on paper first. Then open the possible answer to compare. Your answer need not match. It needs a because and a so.

1. "Students who study in groups score higher."
One possible answer

Because explaining a topic to others shows what you have not understood, group study exposes gaps early, so students fix them before the exam.

2. "H2: Kirana stores that give credit to regular customers keep more of them."
One possible answer

Because credit builds a habit of buying at one store and a duty to come back, customers with credit avoid switching, so retention rises.

3. "Interns who get a mentor receive more job offers."
One possible answer

Because a mentor shows the intern what the employer values and speaks for the intern, the intern performs better on what counts, so offers rise.

All three statements and answers are the teacher's own, made up for practice.

Where to aim

What strong theory looks like

  • It answers why, with the order and timing of causes.
  • It looks down into small processes, sideways to neighboring ideas, and up to larger social patterns.
  • It is built from a simple core, and its arguments link together.
  • It says where the main idea holds most and where it holds least.
  • It can show consequences we would not see unaided.
  • The authors, borrowing a phrase from Weick, say good theory explains, predicts and delights.

The authors admit this reads like a wish list. That is why their article describes what theory is not.
