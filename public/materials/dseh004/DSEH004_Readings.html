DSEH004 · Performance Management · Exam preparation edition

Set readings, week by week

R K Singh · Senior Professor, Department of Commerce · Delhi School of Economics, University of Delhi

These are the set readings from the DSEH004 Lesson Plan for the weeks covered by the exam preparation edition. In house simulation materials are omitted because they are not needed for the examination. Each lecture deck also lists the background sources named on its slides.

Week 1 · Course Foundations: Why Performance Management Is Changing

  • Buckingham, M., & Goodall, A. (2015). Reinventing performance management. Harvard Business Review, 93(4), 40–50.

Week 2 · Engagement Foundations: Kahn, Q12 and Job Demands Resources

  • Kahn, W. A. (1990). Psychological conditions of personal engagement and disengagement at work. Academy of Management Journal, 33(4), 692–724. (Lesson Plan: Sections I to III.)
  • Bhatnagar, J. (2007). Talent management strategy of employee engagement in Indian ITES employees: Key to retention. Employee Relations, 29(6), 640–663.
  • Bakker, A. B., & Demerouti, E. (2017). Job demands–resources theory: Taking stock and looking forward. Journal of Occupational Health Psychology, 22(3), 273–285.

Week 3 · Motivation Theory: Self Determination, Flow, Purpose and Psychological Capital

  • Deci, E. L., & Ryan, R. M. (2008). Self determination theory: A macrotheory of human motivation, development, and health. Canadian Psychology, 49(3), 182–185.
  • Csikszentmihalyi, M. (1990). Flow: The psychology of optimal experience. Harper & Row. Chapters 1 to 2.
  • Pink, D. H. (2009). Drive: The surprising truth about what motivates us. Riverhead Books. Chapters 1 to 3.
  • Luthans, F., Youssef, C. M., & Avolio, B. J. (2007). Psychological capital: Developing the human competitive edge. Oxford University Press. Chapter 1.

Week 4 · From Annual Appraisals to Continuous Performance Management

  • Buckingham, M., & Goodall, A. (2015). Reinventing performance management. Harvard Business Review, 93(4), 40–50. Reread through the design lens.
  • Locke, E. A., & Latham, G. P. (2002). Building a practically useful theory of goal setting and task motivation: A 35 year odyssey. American Psychologist, 57(9), 705–717.
  • Doerr, J. (2018). Measure what matters: How Google, Bono, and the Gates Foundation rock the world with OKRs. Portfolio. Chapters 1 to 3.

Week 5 · Balanced Scorecards, Coaching and Real Time Feedback

  • Kaplan, R. S., & Norton, D. P. (1996). The balanced scorecard: Translating strategy into action. Harvard Business School Press. Chapter 1.
  • Whitmore, J. (2017). Coaching for performance (5th ed.). Nicholas Brealey. Chapter on the GROW model.
  • Cappelli, P., & Tavis, A. (2016). The performance management revolution. Harvard Business Review, 94(10), 58–67.

Week 6 · Trust, Psychological Contracts and Reading Pulse Data

  • Rousseau, D. M. (1995). Psychological contracts in organizations: Understanding written and unwritten agreements. Sage. Introduction.
  • Mayer, R. C., Davis, J. H., & Schoorman, F. D. (1995). An integrative model of organizational trust. Academy of Management Review, 20(3), 709–734.
  • Schaufeli, W. B., & Bakker, A. B. (2004). Job demands, job resources, and their relationship with burnout and engagement: A multi sample study. Journal of Organizational Behavior, 25(3), 293–315. Reread for interpreting pulse data.

Week 7 · Engagement Strategy and Drivers Analysis

  • Saks, A. M. (2006). Antecedents and consequences of employee engagement. Journal of Managerial Psychology, 21(7), 600–619.
  • Macey, W. H., Schneider, B., Barbera, K. M., & Young, S. A. (2009). Employee engagement: Tools for analysis, practice, and competitive advantage. Wiley Blackwell. Chapters 1 to 2.
  • People Matters and SHRM India: a selected case on an Indian engagement transformation (Wipro, Infosys or HCLTech), as listed in the Lesson Plan.

Week 8 · Multigenerational and Lifecycle Engagement

  • Costanza, D. P., Badger, J. M., Fraser, R. L., Severt, J. B., & Gade, P. A. (2012). Generational differences in work related attitudes: A meta analysis. Journal of Business and Psychology, 27(4), 375–394.
  • McKinsey & Company. (2022, July). The Great Attrition is making hiring harder. Are you searching the right talent pools? McKinsey Quarterly.
  • People Matters: selected articles on the employee lifecycle, as listed in the Lesson Plan.
  • Singh, S. K., & Singh, A. P. (2019). Interplay of organizational justice, psychological empowerment, organizational citizenship behavior, and job satisfaction in the context of circular economy. Management Decision, 57(4), 937–952.

Week 9 · Strengths Based Performance Management and Career Mobility

  • Buckingham, M., & Clifton, D. O. (2001). Now, discover your strengths. Free Press. Chapter 1.
  • Cappelli, P. (2008). Talent on demand: Managing talent in an age of uncertainty. Harvard Business Press. Chapter on internal mobility.
  • Hewlett, S. A. (2013). Forget a mentor, find a sponsor: The new way to fast track your career. Harvard Business Review Press. Chapter 1.

Week 10 · Mentoring, Learning and Development, and High Performance Work Systems

  • Eby, L. T., Allen, T. D., Evans, S. C., Ng, T., & DuBois, D. L. (2008). Does mentoring matter? A multidisciplinary meta analysis comparing mentored and non mentored individuals. Journal of Vocational Behavior, 72(2), 254–267.
  • Jennings, C. (2013). 70:20:10 framework explained [Practitioner monograph]. (Listed in the Lesson Plan; year not independently confirmed.)
  • Pfeffer, J. (1998). The human equation: Building profits by putting people first. Harvard Business School Press. Chapter 2.

Week 11 · Recognition and Culture Transformation

  • Brun, J. P., & Dugas, N. (2008). An analysis of employee recognition: Perspectives on human resources practices. International Journal of Human Resource Management, 19(4), 716–730.
  • Edmondson, A. C. (1999). Psychological safety and learning behavior in work teams. Administrative Science Quarterly, 44(2), 350–383.

Week 12 · Course Synthesis and End Term Preparation

  • Kahn, W. A. (1990). Psychological conditions of personal engagement and disengagement at work. Academy of Management Journal, 33(4), 692–724.
  • Bakker, A. B., & Demerouti, E. (2017). Job demands–resources theory: Taking stock and looking forward. Journal of Occupational Health Psychology, 22(3), 273–285.
  • Deci, E. L., & Ryan, R. M. (2008). Self determination theory: A macrotheory of human motivation, development, and health. Canadian Psychology, 49(3), 182–185.
  • Locke, E. A., & Latham, G. P. (2002). Building a practically useful theory of goal setting and task motivation: A 35 year odyssey. American Psychologist, 57(9), 705–717.
  • Buckingham, M., & Goodall, A. (2015). Reinventing performance management. Harvard Business Review, 93(4), 40–50.
  • Pink, D. H. (2009). Drive: The surprising truth about what motivates us. Riverhead Books.
  • Csikszentmihalyi, M. (1990). Flow: The psychology of optimal experience. Harper & Row.
  • Schaufeli, W. B., & Bakker, A. B. (2004). Job demands, job resources, and their relationship with burnout and engagement: A multi sample study. Journal of Organizational Behavior, 25(3), 293–315.
